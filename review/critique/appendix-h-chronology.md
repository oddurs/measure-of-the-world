# Appendix H — Chronologies: fact-check

Checked against `src/appendices/appendix-h.tex` (rev 9cf456c). Ledger: `review/claims/apph.yaml`.

**79 ledger entries checked. 32 wrong (`corrected`), 4 unsupported and left `unverified`, 43 verified.**

Errors cluster in three places: the post-1900 institutional history of the Observatory (almost every entry is wrong), telescope apertures (three off by factors of 2.5 to 25), and the observation-versus-announcement distinction (four entries).

---

## Must fix before print — worst first

### 1. The office no longer belongs to Martin Rees (apph-002, apph-079)

Rees served **1995–2025**. **Michele Dougherty** was appointed in **July 2025**, the sixteenth Astronomer Royal and the first woman to hold the office. The appendix says "1995–present" and counts fifteen holders. Both are now wrong, and the appendix has no sixteenth entry at all.

Consequences that ripple through the aggregate line:

| Claim as printed | Correct |
|---|---|
| 15 Astronomers Royal | 16 |
| Average tenure ≈ 23 years | ≈ 22 years (346 years / 16) |
| Most served 15–30 years | 8 of 16, exactly half |

Source: [Royal Museums Greenwich](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/astronomer-royal); [gov.uk press release, 30 July 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/astronomer-royal-30th-july-2025); [Royal Astronomical Society](https://www.ras.ac.uk/news-and-press/news/michele-dougherty-becomes-first-female-astronomer-royal).

### 2. The 1910 Christie entry contradicts the appendix's own table (apph-030)

The master timeline says Christie **became** Astronomer Royal in 1910. He **left** in 1910, having held the office since 1881. Frank Watson Dyson succeeded him that year. The appendix's own tenure table says "1881–1910 Christie", and chapter 14 says "William Henry Mahoney Christie (1881--1910)". A reader who reads both sections in the same appendix will catch this.

### 3. The 1821 Hipparcos entry describes nothing that exists (apph-016)

> 1821 Hipparcos introduces systematic parallax measurement method

Hipparcos is an ESA satellite launched in 1989. There was no 1821 Hipparcos, and no 1821 event resembling this. Delete it, or substitute a real 1820s milestone such as Bessel's *Fundamenta Astronomiae* (1818) or the founding of the Royal Astronomical Society (1820).

### 4. The Observatory did not move to Herstmonceux in 1981 (apph-043, apph-041)

The move began in **1948** and was completed in **1957**. In 1981 the Royal Greenwich Observatory was already at Herstmonceux and would move to **Cambridge in 1990**. This entry also contradicts the appendix's own Woolley line (1956–1971), which correctly credits him with the relocation.

The companion 1960 entry compounds it: it claims 285 years of *continuous observation* at Greenwich, an Airy Transit Circle "still operational", and a photographic zenith tube at Greenwich. By 1960 the observatory had left Greenwich, the transit circle's last observation was **30 March 1954** (as the appendix's own instrument entry states), and the zenith tube was at Herstmonceux.

Source: [Royal Observatory Greenwich, A Brief History](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1).

### 5. The Isaac Newton Telescope is 98 inches, not 98 cm (apph-043, apph-062)

Off by a factor of 25, and it appears twice. The INT is **2.5 m**, the largest telescope ever sited in the British Isles. At 98 cm it would be an unremarkable instrument, and the sentence about it marking a shift toward modern astrophysics would make no sense.

### 6. Both Herschel apertures are wrong by a factor of 2.5 (apph-015, apph-058, apph-059)

| Instrument | Printed | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 20-foot reflector (1783) | 190 mm | 18.7 in = **475 mm** |
| 40-foot reflector (1789) | 490 mm | 48 in = **1220 mm** |

The 40-foot is called "largest telescope of the era" in the same sentence that gives it a 490 mm mirror, which would have made it smaller than Herschel's own 20-foot.

### 7. The photographic zenith tube is 55 years and one county out of place (apph-028, apph-061)

The design dates from **Frank Ross, 1911**. The Royal Greenwich Observatory's PZT began operating in **1955**, at **Herstmonceux**, and ceased 30 June 1984. Nothing of the kind existed at Greenwich in 1900. Source: [Royal Observatory Greenwich](https://www.royalobservatorygreenwich.org/articles.php?article=1071).

### 8. Hipparcos launched in 1989, not 1980 (apph-063)

Launched **8 August 1989**, operated to March 1993. The catalogue holds 118,218 stars. Source: [ESA fact sheet](https://sci.esa.int/web/hipparcos/-/47357-fact-sheet).

### 9. J2000.0 was adopted in 1976, not 2000 (apph-048)

The IAU adopted the epoch at Grenoble in **1976**; it took effect on **1 January 1984** with the FK5 catalogue, replacing FK4/B1950.0. The entry places it 24 years late, apparently reasoning from the epoch's name.

### 10. Four observation-versus-announcement errors

|---|---|---|
| apph-052 LIGO | 2016 detection | Detected **14 Sept 2015**, announced 11 Feb 2016 |
| apph-008 Bradley aberration | 1725 discovery | Observations begin Dec 1725; **explained 1728**, announced Jan 1729 |
| apph-054 Greenwich anniversary | 350th in 2024 | Warrant of 22 June 1675, so **2025** |
| apph-039 caesium second | 1955 becomes the standard | Clock built 1955; second redefined **1967**; TAI 1970/71 |

## The four replacement Astronomer Royal entries

**All four hold up.** Dates, order and biographical detail are correct against Royal Museums Greenwich and the relevant obituaries. One date needs a second look and one entry is now out of date.

| Entry | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Ryle 1972–1982 (10 years) | **Correct throughout.** First honorary holder; Nobel Prize in Physics 1974 shared with Antony Hewish, cited for aperture synthesis. |
| Graham-Smith 1982–1990 (8 years) | **Tenure correct.** Jodrell Bank 1981–1988 correct. The RGO directorship given as 1975–1981 is disputed; most sources say **1976**–1981. Check against the RAS obituary or Who's Who. |
| Wolfendale 1991–1995 (4 years) | **Correct.** Durham cosmic-ray physicist; President of the Institute of Physics 1994–1996. Worth adding: President of the Royal Astronomical Society 1981–1983. |
| Rees 1995–present | **Tenure now closed at 2025.** Trinity 2004–2012 and Royal Society 2005–2010 are correct; "longest tenure since Airy" holds at 30 years. |

**The account of the 1972 split is correct.** Woolley was the last Astronomer Royal to also direct the Observatory; he retired in 1971, a separate Director took over day-to-day running of the Royal Greenwich Observatory in 1972, and the title became honorary from Ryle onward. Royal Museums Greenwich states this in those terms.

**One entry is missing.** Add Michele Dougherty, 2025–present, and change Rees to 1995–2025.

**All eleven earlier tenures and their stated durations are arithmetically correct**, verified against the Royal Museums Greenwich list: Flamsteed 44, Halley 22, Bradley 20, Bliss 2, Maskelyne 46, Pond 24, Airy 46, Christie 29, Dyson 23, Spencer Jones 22, Woolley 15. "Longest: Maskelyne and Airy, 46 each" and "Shortest: Bliss" are both right, as is "three served over 40 years".

One factual clause in an otherwise correct tenure entry is wrong: **Spencer Jones did not establish Greenwich Civil Time** (apph-074). The term was in use from 1925 and the IAU replaced it with "Universal Time" in 1928, five years before he took office.
